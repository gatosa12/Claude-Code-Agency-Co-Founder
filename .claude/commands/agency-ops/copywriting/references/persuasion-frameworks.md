# Persuasion Frameworks for Ads & Copywriting

Six techniques, each with its structure, when to use it, the common pitfall and how to avoid it, and examples. The generic example comes from the original framework table; the voice AI example shows it applied to an agency offer (AI receptionist / speed-to-lead / database reactivation).

---

## 1. Pain-Agitate-Solve (PAS)

**Explanation:** Identify the problem, deepen the pain, then offer the solution.

**Structure**
1. **Pain** -- name the problem in the audience's own words (1 line)
2. **Agitate** -- show what it costs them: money, time, stress, lost customers (1–2 lines)
3. **Solve** -- present the offer as the specific fix, with a concrete outcome and a CTA (the longest part)

**Common pitfall:** Focusing too much on pain and not enough on solving.
**Avoid it:** Keep Pain + Agitate to about a third of the copy. End on the outcome and the next step, never on the problem.

**Generic example:** "Struggling with weight? We can help!"
> Note: on Meta, "Struggling with weight?" implies a personal attribute and is likely to be rejected. Prefer "Weight-loss plans built for busy schedules."

**Voice AI example**
> Every missed call is a patient booking with the clinic down the road.
> After hours, during lunch, when the front desk is slammed — those calls go to voicemail, and most people never leave one.
> Our AI receptionist answers every call in under a second, books straight into your calendar, and texts you a summary. Book a 15-minute demo and hear it answer your own number.

## 2. AIDA

**Explanation:** Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

**Structure**
1. **Attention** -- a headline or hook that stops the scroll (surprising stat, bold claim you can back, question)
2. **Interest** -- why this matters to them specifically
3. **Desire** -- the benefits and the picture of life after (outcomes, not features)
4. **Action** -- one clear, specific CTA

**Common pitfall:** Missing one of the steps (usually jumping from Attention straight to Action, or ending with a vague CTA).
**Avoid it:** Label each step while drafting, then remove the labels. One CTA only.

**Generic example:** From a headline to a CTA button.

**Voice AI example**
> **A:** 62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered. [Use a real, sourced stat or the client's own call data.]
> **I:** For a home-services company, that's jobs going to whoever picks up first.
> **D:** Imagine every call answered, qualified and booked — at 2am, on Sundays, during your busiest hour — without hiring another person.
> **A:** Get your free missed-call audit →

## 3. FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)

**Explanation:** Leverage the fear of missing out — a deadline, limited availability, or others getting ahead.

**Structure**
1. The opportunity
2. The real constraint (date, number of slots, price change, cohort start)
3. What they lose by waiting
4. CTA tied to the constraint

**Common pitfall:** Overuse, making it lose impact.
**Avoid it:** Use FOMO once per piece, usually in the CTA. The constraint must be true and specific — "3 onboarding slots left for December" beats "Limited offer, act now!" If there is no real constraint, use **cost of inaction** instead ("Every week without it is ~40 missed calls").

**Generic example:** "Limited offer, act now!"

**Voice AI example**
> We onboard 4 new clinics a month so every launch gets a dedicated build. December has 2 slots left. [Only if true.]
> Clinics that start this month go live before the January rush. Grab a slot →

## 4. Social Proof

**Explanation:** Use testimonials, ratings, case studies, client logos, user numbers.

**Structure**
1. The proof (quote, number, rating, recognisable name)
2. Who it comes from — someone like the reader (same niche, size, problem)
3. The specific result
4. CTA ("get the same result")

**Common pitfall:** Using fake or unrelatable testimonials.
**Avoid it:** Only use real, approved proof from the user or context files. Match the proof to the audience (a dental testimonial for dental ads). Specific beats vague: "Recovered 31 no-show appointments in 30 days" beats "Great service!". If there is no proof yet, write `[REAL TESTIMONIAL NEEDED]` and suggest the testimonial-collection SOP in `context/sops/testimonial-collection.md`.

**Generic example:** "99% of users recommend us!"
> Note: a stat like this needs a real source and survey behind it, or it reads as fake.

**Voice AI example**
> "We were missing about 30 calls a week. In the first month the AI booked 19 new patients we would have lost." — [Name], Practice Manager, [Real Clinic]
> See how it would work for your practice →

## 5. Emotional Appeal

**Explanation:** Target emotions rather than logic — relief, pride, freedom, security, belonging.

**Structure**
1. Name the feeling they want (or want to escape)
2. Connect it to a concrete moment in their life
3. Show the offer as the bridge to that feeling
4. CTA

**Common pitfall:** Being overly sentimental.
**Avoid it:** Anchor every emotion to a concrete, believable outcome. One emotion per piece. Cut clichés ("dream life", "transform your world"). Let a small specific image do the work.

**Generic example:** "Find your freedom, travel!"

**Voice AI example**
> Imagine closing the shop at 6 and actually leaving work at work.
> No checking voicemail at dinner. No calling back leads who already hired someone else.
> Your AI receptionist handles the phone so you can be home on time. Hear it in action →

## 6. Storytelling

**Explanation:** Use a story to sell the product.

**Structure** (short form for ads: 3–5 sentences)
1. **Hero** -- a real person like the reader, with a name and a role
2. **Problem** -- the moment things were going wrong
3. **Turning point** -- they found the offer
4. **Result** -- a specific, believable outcome
5. **Invitation** -- CTA for the reader to have the same story

**Common pitfall:** Making the story too long or irrelevant.
**Avoid it:** Start in the middle of the problem, not with background. The hero must look like the audience, and the product must be the turning point (not a side detail). For ads, keep to 5 sentences or 30 seconds of video. Use real stories only, with permission; label composite or illustrative stories clearly.

**Generic example:** "Meet Sarah, a satisfied customer..."

**Voice AI example**
> Mark runs a 3-van plumbing business. Last winter he lost a £4,000 boiler job because nobody answered at 7am.
> In January he switched on an AI receptionist. It answered the next emergency call in one ring and had the job booked before Mark finished his coffee.
> Want your phone answered like that? Book a demo →
> [Replace with a real client story, with permission.]

## Combining Techniques

| Combo | How |
| --- | --- |
| PAS + Social Proof | Pain → Agitate → Solve → one real testimonial as evidence |
| Storytelling + FOMO | Story for the body, real deadline in the CTA |
| AIDA + Social Proof | Use a proof stat as the Attention hook |
| Emotional + PAS | Agitate with the emotional cost, Solve with the feeling regained |

Rule of thumb: one **body** technique (PAS, AIDA, Storytelling, Emotional) + at most one **booster** (Social Proof, FOMO).

## Platform Notes

| Platform | Limits / guidance | Best techniques |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta / Instagram ads | Primary text ~125 chars before "See more"; headline ~40 chars. No personal-attribute phrasing ("Are you…?", "Your debt…") | PAS, Social Proof, Storytelling |
| Google search ads | Headlines 30 chars (up to 15), descriptions 90 chars (up to 4). Match search intent | AIDA (compressed), Social Proof |
| LinkedIn | First 2 lines show before "…see more" — hook must land there | Storytelling, Social Proof |
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | Hook in first 1–2 seconds; 15–45s scripts; on-screen text | PAS, Storytelling, Emotional |
| YouTube ads | Hook before the 5-second skip | AIDA, PAS |
| Cold email | Subject under ~6 words, body under ~100 words, one CTA, no hype | PAS, Social Proof |
| Landing page | Hero = Attention + Interest; sections = Desire; repeat one CTA | AIDA with Social Proof blocks |

Character limits change — confirm against the platform's current ad specs before launching.

## Ethics & Compliance Checklist

- [ ] Every testimonial, rating, number and client name is real and approved
- [ ] Every deadline or scarcity claim is true
- [ ] No personal-attribute targeting language on Meta
- [ ] Health, finance, legal and earnings claims are substantiated or removed
- [ ] Composite or illustrative stories are labelled as such
- [ ] Copy matches the brand voice in `context/agency.md`
